\begin{frame}{Life Cycle vs Beliefs?}

Two competing hypotheses: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Lifecycle: currently low  income + time discounting, present financial stress, long-term career/lifestyle risk, prospect of upward mobility. 
    
    \vspace{0.2em}
    Largely \textit{self-interest}-driven.
    \item Belief divergence: experienced inequality, growth, mobility, housing prices, social safety net access, diversity, immigration. Shaped by political narratives and the supply of politicians/platforms.
    % (who to blame) (electoral institutions)

    \vspace{0.2em}
    Largely \textit{belief}-driven.
    % Real economic conditions 
\end{itemize}
    
\end{frame}



\begin{frame}{Summary of findings}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item It is not the economic position of the young: 26 measures of income, risk and prospects explain none of the gap.
    \item It is what the young believe about the economy: zero-sum and deservingness beliefs account for much of the gap.
    \item The age gap on redistribution grew from zero in the 1970s–80s to +0.5 SD today (half of the party gap, similar to the race gap, double the gender gap).
    \item A similar age gap in beliefs has emerged. 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Ongoing analysis: what drives these beliefs?
    \end{itemize}
    % the US young-old gap in support for redistribution
\end{enumerate}
\end{frame}



\begin{frame}{Our survey}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Online survey of U.S. citizens, fielded on Prolific in spring 2025.
    \item $N = 2,415$, one half 18--30, one half $>30$.
    \item 15 minutes on average, 92 questions.
    \item Covers current financial position, history, expectations, risks, beliefs about the economy, and values.
    % \item Benchmarked against external surveys: \cite{chinoy2026zero}, GSS, ANES, and WVS/EVS.
    % government should reduce income differences, federal tax progressivity, and support for a revenue-neutral tax-shift proposal.
    % \item Built to test the two hypotheses head-to-head: 26 self-interest constructs (income, financial position, long-term risk, upward-mobility prospects, time preference) against beliefs and values (zero-sum, attribution/deservingness, fairness).
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Questions asked}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Policy preferences: income redistribution (3), why preferences changed (2), alternative policies to support low-income (5).
    \item Economic position: current and expected income (12), future income needs (5), financial insecurity (4), AI/climate/housing/health risk (4), family/personal history (5), ambitions (5).
    \item Beliefs: incentive effects of taxation (2), zero-sum beliefs (4), mobility (4).
    \item Values: time discounting (7), generosity/universalism (2), materialism (2).

\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Discussion: is it zero-sum or deservingness?}

Related but distinct beliefs
\begin{itemize}
    \item Positive-sum vs zero-sum: whether one's effort can grow the pie or one's gains are necessarily another's losses. 
    % distribution
    \item Attribution/deservingness: whether one's gains were deserved or earned unfairly. %(reflecting growing inequality).
\end{itemize}
Observations
\begin{itemize}
    \item Young people on average believe that CEOs are zero-sum but not DEI initiatives; zero-sum is not a universal belief.
    \item ``Tax demotivates'' and ``gap motivates'' address positive-sumness and predict preferences but have no age gap. 
\end{itemize}
    
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The US age gap is new. 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item It started emerging in the 80s and widened substantially around 2020--24.
    \end{itemize}
    \item It is not the economic position of the young, but whether they believe the economic game is zero-sum or rigged. 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Other academic surveys tend to agree.
        % \item Zero-sum and deservingness are different beliefs with different age gaps.
    \end{itemize}
    \item The US age gap is near the top of 91 countries, with the UK and the Nordics. 
    \item Where the young are more zero-sum, they tend to be more pro-redistribution, across and within countries.
\end{itemize}
    
\end{frame}
